Overlay one map layer on another

A map overlay adds one geographic layer on top of another, helping you compare related information in the same place. In the sample configuration, satellite imagery is paired with a railway layer. The imagery provides visible ground context, while the railway layer highlights mapped infrastructure. An opacity control lets you adjust how strongly the overlay appears in the workspace.

Dualmaps also supports synchronized side-by-side comparison. Use the layer selector for the left map and the provider selector for the right map to choose views that suit your question. Available layers include street, topographic, cycling, satellite, railway and LIDAR styles. Overlay data and base maps come from different providers, so their coverage, visual detail, update frequency and terms may differ.

Example: railway data over satellite imagery

Open the Berlin example to view a railway overlay in relation to satellite imagery. Increase or reduce the overlay opacity to balance the linework with the image beneath it. Zoom toward a station or rail corridor to inspect local context, then pan along the route while the other map stays aligned.

This visual technique can help with orientation and exploratory research, but it does not confirm that infrastructure is active, accessible or current. For authoritative or time-sensitive decisions, consult the official data provider. If the overlay is too busy for a particular location, switch to a simpler layer or compare the two sources side by side instead.

Choose a useful map combination

Try satellite imagery with a street or cycling map, a relief layer with roads, or railway information alongside a standard map. The best combination depends on the feature you want to understand. Use the overlay selector to turn a layer on or off, and use the opacity setting to make the underlying view easier to read.
